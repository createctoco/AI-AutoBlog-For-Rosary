## Materials and Craftsmanship

Construction is deliberately plain. Rounded wooden beads are strung on elastic cord, so the bracelet slips on without a clasp — a detail that matters when you're packing hundreds of pieces for distribution, because there's no small metal component to break or tangle. The cross pendant is carved from wood in a matching brown tone and attached securely to the strand.

Natural wood is the headline material, and it behaves like natural wood. Grain patterns and shade vary slightly from bead to bead and from batch to batch, which is part of the charm but also something to plan for. If your customer base expects identical color across 500 units, say so early and ask what tolerance is realistic. Nobody wants a shipment dispute over wood tone that was never going to be uniform.

Durability is adequate for daily wear and devotional use. The finish is chosen to keep a natural look rather than a lacquered shine, so the piece ages instead of degrading — a solid talking point for buyers targeting an older or more traditional demographic.

Behind the product sits a manufacturing setup built for volume. Mecrt handles independent design, handmade production, and OEM/ODM work under one roof, with a stable artisan team of more than 160 craftspeople specializing in traditional Peruvian knot techniques. Monthly output exceeds 100,000 pieces, and annual production capacity runs above USD 12 million. That scale matters if you're planning a seasonal push and need consistent replenishment rather than a one-off lot.

For buyers who get asked about sourcing ethics by their own customers, there's another angle: the company works with rural low-income artisan groups as part of its production model, creating local jobs in the handmade supply chain.
