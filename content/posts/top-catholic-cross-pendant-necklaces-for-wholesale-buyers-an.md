## What to Confirm with the Supplier

Specifications first. The source material confirms alloy construction, polished gold finish, beaded chain and collarbone length, but it doesn't publish exact chain length in centimeters, pendant dimensions, or piece weight. Ask for those numbers in writing — they affect how the item displays, how it ships, and whether it fits your packaging.

Then plating and finish durability. Gold-tone plating on alloy varies by process, and this is the single most common source of customer complaints in the category. Ask how the finish is applied and what wear guidance the supplier recommends.

Sampling is the next step worth budgeting for. Request a sample before a bulk commitment, ideally more than one unit so you can check consistency across pieces — bead alignment, chain drape, clasp function, cross detailing. A single sample tells you what the item can be; several tell you what it usually is.

On logistics, confirm MOQ against your chosen configuration, lead time for both stock and OEM orders, and how packaging customization affects the schedule. None of these are published figures, so treat any number you haven't been given directly as unconfirmed.

Finally, clarify labeling and compliance requirements for your market. Ask what documentation the supplier can provide for your destination country and what's outside their scope. Better to find that out at inquiry stage than at the port.
