### The Sweet Spot: Size, Material, and Design

Babies are small. That sounds obvious, but you'd be surprised how many suppliers send samples with crosses the size of a man's thumb. No good.

The cross itself should be no bigger than 1.5 inches. Chain length? 14 to 16 inches is standard. Anything longer and it gets tangled. Anything shorter and it's a choker — not appropriate for an infant.

We use **[solid brass or sterling silver](https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/Catholic-Rosary-Crucifix-Religious-Jewelry-Saint_1601126619552.html)** for most of our tiny crosses. Gold plating or silver plating over brass keeps the cost down without looking cheap. The chain should be thin — 1mm to 1.5mm thickness. Think delicate, not flimsy.

### What About the Chain? Flat Knot vs. Peruvian Knot

Here's a detail most catalog descriptions skip: the chain connection.

Some of our necklaces use a flat knot to attach the cross to the chain. Others use a Peruvian knot. What's the difference?

Flat knot lies flush against the chain. Looks clean, modern. Peruvian knot has a bit more texture — it's a traditional knot that doesn't slip. Both are strong. Both work.

We let the buyer decide. Some churches have a preference. Some don't care. We've done thousands of pieces with both styles.

For infant necklaces specifically, we recommend the flat knot. It's less likely to catch on clothing or get pulled loose by tiny fingers.
