## Design and Craft Details

The iconography is traditional and deliberately plain. Cross, medal, and patron pendants all carry a clean devotional finish rather than ornamental flourishes — a look that travels across age groups and price points. Nothing about the design needs a salesperson to explain it, which is exactly what you want at a counter display.

## Questions to Ask Before Ordering

Spec sheets for metal religious goods are often thinner than buyers expect, so pin the details down in writing before you approve a run.

Start with the bead specification: material, color options, and whether the linked chain and connector hardware match the pendant finish. Plating consistency is the next thing to lock, since a batch that reads silver in one carton and grey in the next looks like two products on the same shelf.

Then the pendant itself. Confirm the exact motifs per assortment, whether miracle medal and St. Benedict pieces are mixed or packed separately, and how the relief compares at 1:1 scale. Ask for a pre-production sample on any custom configuration — photographs compress metal detail and always flatter it.

Packaging deserves its own round of questions. Bulk poly bag, individual pouch, hanging header card, or retail-ready box each changes both your landed cost and your labor cost at the warehouse. If parish bookstores are your channel, a header card is often non-negotiable.

On commercial terms, clarify which MOQ tier applies to your specific configuration, how mixed assortments affect unit pricing, and what volume pricing tiers exist for larger commitments. Mecrt handles quotation requests directly rather than publishing a fixed price list, so the numbers you receive should reflect your actual mix. Lead times, carton dimensions, and shipping terms vary by item — contact us to confirm before you build a delivery schedule around assumptions.

Sourcing for a diocese with multiple parish accounts? Ask about consolidated shipments and whether one order can be split by destination. It's a small question that saves a lot of freight later.
