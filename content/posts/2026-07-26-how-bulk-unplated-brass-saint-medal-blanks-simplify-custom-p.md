## Selecting Unplated Brass Saint Medal Blanks for Custom Plating: Key Considerations for Wholesale Buyers

When sourcing saint medal blanks for a custom plating service, the choice of raw material and supplier directly affects your final product’s quality, cost, and turnaround. Unplated raw brass blanks offer maximum flexibility: you can apply any plating finish—gold, silver, antique bronze, or rhodium—without the limitations of pre-finished stock. For church procurement officers, importers, and religious goods wholesalers, the decision involves balancing inventory risk, customization requirements, and minimum order quantities.

### Understanding the Raw Brass Blank: What to Look For

Raw brass saint medal blanks are typically stamped or cast from sheet brass, then left untreated for later plating. The key specifications to confirm with your supplier include:

- **Metal thickness and gauge** – Thicker blanks hold detail better and feel more substantial, but cost more. Thinner blanks reduce weight and per-unit cost for high-volume orders. We do not publish standard gauges for our blanks; availability varies by item, so please confirm the exact thickness with us for the specific medal shape you need.
- **Diameter and shape** – Common saint medal diameters range from 1/2 inch to 1 inch, but not every size is stocked. For bulk unplated blanks, verify that your desired size is available in the raw state before committing to a plating run.
- **Edge finish and surface quality** – A clean, burr-free edge prevents plating defects. While we do not specify surface roughness, our brass blanks are inspected for uniform finish. Ask about the blank’s origin (domestic vs. imported) if that affects your lead time or customs considerations.

Because unplated blanks are intermediate goods, any deviation in the raw material will be magnified after plating. Request a sample of the blank in its current state before ordering in volume. This is especially important if you plan to add a church name or parish logo via engraving or embossing.

### Customization Options: Logo, Parish Name, and Packaging

For buyers who intend to offer personalized saint medals—such as for First Communion, Confirmation, or parish jubilees—the path from blank to finished product often involves custom engraving, embossing, or printing on the medal itself or on its accompanying tag or packaging.

Our business supports OEM/custom-logo orders at a minimum quantity of 1,200 pieces. This MOQ applies to any product where we apply a logo, church name, or parish name on eligible tags or packaging. For medals that require custom die-struck faces (e.g., a specific saint image with a church name), the MOQ may differ; please confirm the specific item and customization method with us before ordering.

Important tradeoff: If you need only a few hundred custom medals for a one-time event, consider using our in-stock saint medals (MOQ 12 pieces) and adding a custom prayer card or ribbon instead. This avoids the higher OEM MOQ while still meeting the devotional purpose. For ongoing annual events, the 1,200-piece OEM order becomes cost-effective, especially if you can spread inventory across multiple years or share a production run with other parishes.

### Plating Decisions: Matching Finish to Use

The raw brass blank is your canvas. The plating you choose should align with both the devotional context and the wear the item will receive.

- **Gold or yellow gold plate** – Traditional for high-grade rosary accessories and altar medals. It resists tarnish well but requires a proper base layer (often nickel or copper) to avoid discoloration. Confirm with your plater whether the blank’s brass composition is compatible with your intended gold plating process.
- **Silver plate** – Common for saint medals given at First Communion or Confirmation. Silver plate can be bright or oxidized (antique silver). Note that silver tarnishes more quickly, especially in humid climates. A protective lacquer coating is recommended if the medals will be worn daily.
- **Antique bronze or copper** – Favored for memorial or serendipity-style jewelry, often paired with wooden rosary beads. If you are matching these medals to a line of wooden rosaries, our relevant wooden rosaries use natural solid wood (not plastic or imitation wood) and hand-polished beads. Before committing to a plating finish, ask your plater for a small test run on one blank. Check adhesion, color consistency, and resistance to handling.
