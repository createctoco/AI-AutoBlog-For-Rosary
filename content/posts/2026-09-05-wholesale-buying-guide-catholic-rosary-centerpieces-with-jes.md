## What to Confirm with the Supplier

A product page isn't a specification sheet. Before committing to a bulk order, pin down the details that will determine whether this centerpiece fits your assembly process, pricing, and customer expectations.

Confirm the exact metal composition and finish type. The product is described as durable metal with a polished finish, but your purchasing team will want the specific alloy and plating details to evaluate durability, tarnish resistance, and any compliance documentation your market requires. Also request precise dimensions and per-piece weight. If you're assembling rosaries, a heavy or oversized centerpiece changes the drape and feel of the finished beads.

Order a physical sample before you scale. Relief detail and polish quality don't always translate through product photography, and this is an item where tactile quality drives buying decisions at the parish level.

Clarify how MOQ tiers, lead times, and pricing work across the two order paths — ready-to-ship inventory versus bespoke production. The 12-piece in-stock threshold is a fine testing tool, but your peak-season demand (think Easter, First Communion season, and Christmas) needs a different conversation about capacity.

Don't forget the operational details that come after product selection. Ask about their quality-check process for surface defects and how they handle finish inconsistency in large shipments.
