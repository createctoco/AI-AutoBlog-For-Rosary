We’ve been seeing a steady uptick in orders for Orthodox-style wooden standing crucifixes lately. Not just from Eastern Rite parishes, but from Latin Rite churches too. People want something solid, something that feels rooted in tradition. A crucifix that stands on a table, a side altar, or a prayer corner — not hung on a wall, but *present*.

## What Makes Our Standing Crucifixes Different

We work with solid wood. Real solid wood. Not the resin-and-sawdust composite stuff that looks okay from three feet away but feels hollow in the hand. Our crucifixes are carved from natural timber — oak, walnut, sometimes olive wood from the Holy Land if the client requests it. Each piece is handcrafted.

That’s a detail we don’t gloss over. Because when you’re ordering for a parish, you’re not buying disposable décor. You’re buying something that will sit in a sanctuary, a chapel, or a home for years. Decades, maybe. Machine-pressed crosses just don’t carry the same weight — literally or symbolically.

The standing base is carved separately and fitted by hand. No glue-and-pray-it-holds approach. We cut a precise slot, anchor the cross, and reinforce it so it stands steady. You can bump the table, move it to a different room, pack it for a retreat — it won’t wobble.

## Size, Style, and Practical Considerations

Most churches ask for standing crucifixes in the 12-to-18-inch range. Too small and it disappears on a large altar. Too big and it dominates the space awkwardly. The 14-inch height seems to hit the sweet spot for most orders.

We can do variations, of course. Some clients want a more Byzantine look — the slanted footrest, the double crossbar, the Greek letters. Others prefer a simpler Western-style corpus on an Eastern-style base. We’ve done custom blends. It’s not a problem.

One thing we’ve noticed: parishes ordering for Lenten devotions or mission retreats often ask for the crucifix without a corpus. Just the cross, with the crown of thorns carved into the wood. That’s a popular request. We can do that too.

## The Knots, the Carving, the Little Things

We tie our rope crucifixes with Peruvian knots mostly. Some clients ask for flat knots. Depends on the look they want. Peruvian knots hold tighter and have a cleaner finish in our opinion. But flat knots are easier to adjust if someone wants to swap the crucifix for a different style later.

Each bead on our rosaries is hand-polished. Same with the crucifix wood. We don’t use spray-on varnish. It’s hand-rubbed oil finish. Takes longer, but the grain shows through better. You can actually feel the texture of the wood.

And the carving on the corpus? That’s done with chisels, not CNC routers. You can see slight variations in the expression of the face, the position of the hands. No two are exactly identical. That matters to some buyers. To others, it doesn’t. But we’ve never had a complaint about quality.
