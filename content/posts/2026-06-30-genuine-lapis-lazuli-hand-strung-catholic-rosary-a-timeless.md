## What Makes a Lapis Lazuli Rosary “Real”?

Real lapis has a deep, slightly uneven blue — almost like a night sky with tiny flecks of gold (pyrite) and maybe a white calcite vein here and there. It’s a semi-precious stone, mined mostly in Afghanistan and Chile. And it’s been used in Christian art for centuries. You’ll see it in medieval altar pieces, in the vestments of high-ranking clergy, and in prayer beads passed down through generations.

Our lapis beads are cut and polished individually. Not machine-tumbled in a barrel with a thousand other stones. Each bead gets handled, drilled, and strung by hand. That’s the difference between something that looks like a toy and something that feels sacred.

We’ve been getting a lot of orders from cathedral gift shops and religious goods distributors who want something that actually looks premium — not the cheap resin stuff with a sticker that says “lapis.” If you’re buying for a parish, a retreat center, or a diocesan gift shop, the quality has to reflect the reverence of the object.

## How to Tell If Your Supplier Is Selling Real Lapis

Here’s a quick test. Real lapis is dense. It feels heavy for its size. If you hold a lapis bead next to a glass bead of the same diameter, the lapis will be noticeably heavier. Also, run it under water — real lapis won’t change color. Dyed howlite? It’ll bleed blue like a cheap tie-dye shirt.

We’ve seen suppliers in other countries sell “lapis” that’s actually reconstituted stone dust pressed into beads. That stuff chips and fades within months. Our lapis is solid stone. It’s been used in Catholic devotion for over a thousand years. It should last that long in your inventory.
