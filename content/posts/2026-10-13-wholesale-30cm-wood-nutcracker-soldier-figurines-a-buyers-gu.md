## Specs and Build Quality

Start with material. This is solid wood — not resin, not composition board. That choice drives the weight, the feel in hand, and, more to the point for a sourcing professional, how it survives being packed, shipped, unpacked, displayed for six weeks, packed again, and stored until next December. Seasonal decor gets handled roughly. Wood takes that better than most substitutes, and it's a genuine differentiator when a buyer is comparing two visually similar figurines.

The 30cm height gives it presence. It reads clearly from a shop window or a display table without needing a riser, and it's large enough to anchor a small vignette rather than disappear into one.

Then there's the finish, which is the interesting part. Every piece is hand-painted, and Mecrt is upfront that this produces subtle variations in hue and line from unit to unit. Some buyers instinctively read that as inconsistency. It isn't — it's the artisan signature, and it's precisely what customers hunting vintage, tactile holiday decor respond to. If your channel needs near-identical pieces across a run, raise it early. A hand-painted line behaves differently from a printed one, and that's a conversation to have before the order, not after.

Construction follows the classic nutcracker form: tall bearskin hat, braided coat, ceremonial beard, traditional festive coloring. Nothing's modernized or stylized away. For traditional Christmas displays and gift shops serving customers who want nostalgia over novelty, that restraint is the selling point.

## Checklist Before You Commit

A few things to settle before you sign off.

**Request a sample.** Photos won't tell you how the hand-painting reads at arm's length, how the wood feels, or whether 30cm suits your shelf plan. Get a physical unit in the finish you intend to order.

**Confirm the spec in writing.** Height, material, finish, and any customization. If your market has requirements around painted finishes or material documentation, raise them during sampling — availability varies by item, and it's easier to solve early.

**Match packaging to your channel.** Retail-ready, brown-box bulk, or something between. State it upfront; packaging is one of the customization areas Mecrt supports, but it has to be agreed at quotation.

**Match MOQ to your actual plan.** Testing a market? The 12-piece tier makes sense. Launching a private-label line? Budget for 1,200 and plan sell-through around that.

**Ask about lead times and reorders.** Lead times vary by quantity and customization. If this becomes a recurring Christmas SKU, you'll want the reorder cycle mapped out now.

**Scan the wider catalogue.** If you already buy rosaries, medals or other religious goods, consolidating reduces supplier overhead and shipping complexity.
