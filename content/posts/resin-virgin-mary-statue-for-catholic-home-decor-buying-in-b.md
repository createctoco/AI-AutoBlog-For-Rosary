## Start With the Setting, Then Judge the Statue

Buyers of resin Virgin Mary statues for Catholic decor usually begin with a product photo. The photo shows what the piece looks like under studio light. It does not show how the figure will read in a church vestibule, a school lobby, a gift-shop window, or a family living room. Fix the setting before you compare quotations, because the setting determines which questions actually matter.

Three answers will shape your shortlist: who will see the statue, who will handle it, and how it will travel from your warehouse to the end user. A figure that serves as a devotional focal point in a small chapel has different requirements from one that sits boxed on a retail shelf. A piece placed at child height in a parish hall needs a stable base; one displayed behind glass can favor finer sculptural detail over rough handling. Write the answers down before you contact suppliers.

### Indoor Shelves, Vestibules, and High-Traffic Areas

Most Catholic decor purchases are indoor purchases, but "indoor" covers very different conditions. A shelf in a rectory office, a pedestal in a narthex, and a display table in a parish gift shop each raise a distinct set of checks.

For shelf placement, confirm the footprint depth as well as the height. A tall figure with a wide base may not fit the shelf you had in mind, and shelf dimensions are not something you want to discover at delivery. For floor and pedestal placement, ask about base stability and weight distribution, and whether the figure is solid or hollow, since a light casting is easier to knock over in a busy entrance. For display areas that are cleaned frequently, look at how much dust the sculpting will collect in deep folds and crevices, and how the finish responds to a soft cloth.

Dimensions, weight, and base construction vary by item. Ask us to confirm the specification for the exact SKU you are considering rather than relying on a category assumption.

## Resin Detail, Weight, and Finish: What to Confirm

Resin is used for religious statuary because it captures fine sculptural detail, and because it is lighter than stone or plaster at a comparable size, which affects freight and handling. Those are properties of the material itself. What matters for a purchase order is how a given supplier applies them, so treat each of the following as a written question rather than an assumption.

Ask whether the figure is solid or hollow, and what it weighs. Ask how the finish is applied and whether it is sealed. Hand-painted features, spray coating, and stained washes produce different results on faces and hands, and the difference is often most visible at close range, which is exactly where a home display sits. Ask about color consistency across a batch, particularly if you are supplying several parishes or several retail locations and want the pieces to read as a set. Ask whether the base is molded as part of the figure or attached separately.

Two tradeoffs are worth weighing openly. A heavier casting tends to feel more substantial to the end buyer and resists tipping, but it raises freight cost per unit and changes how you plan cartons. Elaborate surface detail gives you a more expressive figure, but it creates more places for dust to settle and takes more time to clean in a busy church. Decide which side of each tradeoff your channel favors before you ask for pricing.

## A Pre-Order Checklist for Parish and Wholesale Buyers

Convert your questions into a single list and send it with every quotation request. Suppliers who answer in writing tend to be easier to work with later. The list should cover:

- Height, width, depth, weight, and base dimensions for the exact SKU
- Solid or hollow construction, and how the figure stands
- Finish method, sealer, and available color treatments
- Whether the item is intended for indoor display only
- Packaging: individual box, foam or molded insert, inner carton quantity, master carton
- Whether a tag or label can be attached, and under what order terms
- Whether you will receive a sample or pre-production unit before a large run
- Any documentation your diocese or diocesan procurement office requires

Raise documentation requirements at the quotation stage, not after the order is placed. If your buyer is a parish rather than a distributor, the person approving the purchase may need internal sign-off, and a written specification sheet makes that approval faster.

## MOQs, Custom Tags, and Companion Items for Gift Programs

Two order types carry two numbers. In-stock orders start at 12 pieces, which is useful for testing a new market, filling a small parish request, or checking finish quality before committing further. OEM and custom-logo orders start at 1200 pieces. A logo, church name, or parish name can be customized on eligible tags or packaging for OEM orders, and eligibility varies by item, so confirm the specific piece before you plan artwork.

Custom packaging ties a statue to a parish anniversary, a dedication, or a First Communion program. The 1200-piece threshold is a real commitment, so use the 12-piece in-stock option to validate demand and finish first, then move to OEM when the volume is justified.

Statues are usually display items; rosaries are usually distribution items. A parish program that places a statue on the welcome table often hands out rosaries alongside it, and Catholic rosaries may be used for baptism, First Communion, Confirmation, weddings, memorials, daily prayer, and rosary groups. Wooden rosaries for that purpose use natural solid wood rather than plastic or resin imitation wood, and the beads are hand polished. Common bead sizes are 6 mm, 8 mm, 10 mm, and 12 mm, with availability varying by item. For cord rosaries, Peruvian knots and flat knots are available for relevant items; confirm the selected item before ordering.
