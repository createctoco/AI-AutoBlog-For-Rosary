## Questions to Ask Before Ordering

Before you place any bulk order, a few details are worth confirming.

Request a physical sample first. You want to feel the plating, run the beads through your fingers, and experience the scent for yourself. Fragrance strength is subjective — what seems pleasant in a warehouse might feel faint by the time it reaches a display rack. Get one in hand before you commit to volume.

Then confirm the specifications that aren't spelled out in the product description. Exact bead count, cord material, metal type, plating finish, wood variety, and overall length are all worth clarifying. The description tells you the plating is durable, but it doesn't tell you the base metal or finish thickness — those are details you should pin down with the supplier to match your market's expectations.

Ask about lead times as well. They'll depend on quantity, season, and whether you're ordering stock or custom pieces. If you're buying for Easter, first communion season, or Mother's Day, don't assume anything about the production schedule — request dates and work backward from your promo calendar.

Scent durability is another legitimate question. Ask whether the fragrance is infused or surface-applied, how long it typically lasts, and whether unscented versions are available. You don't want a customer returning an item because the rose scent faded after a month. Knowing the answer upfront lets you market the product honestly.

If you're pursuing the custom route, clarify packaging expectations. Mecrt will discuss custom packaging and branding with you directly, but specifics like box materials, printing, and assembly get confirmed order by order. And always request a formal quote with your projected quantities so you can see where bulk pricing tiers land. MOQs are only the starting point of that conversation.

There's also a human story behind the supplier. Mecrt was founded in 2015 and works with rural low-income artisan groups, supporting communities through sustainable handmade industry development. For church-focused buyers, that social dimension can fit your own sourcing narrative — and it's worth asking how they document it if you plan to mention it to your customers.
