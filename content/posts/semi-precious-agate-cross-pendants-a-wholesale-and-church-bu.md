## Construction and Materials

Natural agate is a variety of chalcedony, and the stone's banding and veining are what give this pendant its character. Each piece gets a smooth, polished surface, while the cross silhouette retains natural, rough-hewn edges. That contrast — polished against unpolished, precise against organic — is the whole design idea, and it's what separates the piece on a display tray from a plain cast cross. Buyers who work in natural stone will recognize the logic immediately.

Weight is the second factor. These are lightweight pendants, which matters if you're selling into everyday-wear channels or shipping in volume where grams per unit add up across a carton.

Then there's color. Multiple colors are available, and because the raw material is natural, veining and tone shift from piece to piece. Some of your customers will want exactly that — one-of-a-kind stone is the selling point. Others will expect a consistent shade across a batch. Decide which buyer you're serving before you place the order, because it shapes how you spec the assortment.

## Pre-Order Verification

A natural-stone product can't be judged from a catalog photo alone. The 12-piece in-stock minimum makes sampling straightforward — order a small assortment, handle the pieces, and look at how the veining actually reads under your own lighting. Photographs flatten stone.

Confirm the color assortment ratio before bulk production. "Multiple colors available" doesn't tell you what percentage of each shade ships in a mixed lot, so ask for the breakdown in writing and agree on an acceptable variance range for tone and pattern. Natural material means no two pieces match; your return terms should reflect that reality in plain language.

Ask for a specification sheet covering dimensions, unit weight, attachment type, and finish, and request the packaging options in writing — bulk poly bags versus retail-ready presentation changes both your landed cost and your labor on the receiving end.

Wondering whether a trial shipment is overkill? For first orders with a new supplier, it isn't. It confirms packing quality, breakage rates in transit, and whether the stone arrives looking like the sample. Lead times vary by order size and customization, so confirm them for your specific quantity rather than planning around a general figure.

One more thing worth clarifying early: whether your logo can be applied to this item or only to the broader OEM range. That answer affects your MOQ and your timeline.
