## Checklist Before You Commit

Before you send that inquiry, here's what you should have ready and what you should ask for. This is the part where you protect your margin.

First, request the detailed specifications. Ask for exact dimensions, weight per piece, and the specific alloy composition. The listing tells you it's a durable metal alloy, but you'll want to confirm the exact material — because that affects both price and how the piece holds up in different climates and use conditions.

Second, ask about the finish. The page describes a smooth, modern finish, but you'll want to know whether there's a coating or anti-tarnish treatment. If you're selling to church supply stores, keep in mind that some buyers expect a certain look — matte, polished, antique, or bright. Confirm the available options before assuming.

Third, pin down the packaging. The listing mentions packaging variations are available for bulk orders. If you're selling through retail display, the packaging is part of the product. Ask what the standard packaging looks like and what custom options cost at your target volume.

Fourth, clarify lead times. The source doesn't specify production or shipping timelines, so that's a question for Mecrt's team. Your reorder schedule will depend on whether you're dealing with stock items, which usually move faster, or OEM production, which almost always takes longer.

Check the weight, the finish, the feel of the attachment point. Test it against a zipper pull or a keyring. If you're sourcing for a church gift shop, put it next to other devotional products you already carry and see if it holds its own.
