## Specs and Build Quality

Here's what's actually being offered. The pendant is a hollow cross — the interior is cut away, which reduces weight without shrinking the visual footprint. For a men's piece that's a genuine advantage: you get the presence of a larger cross on the chest, but the pendant stays light enough for all-day wear. The chain is described as durable, and the build targets comfortable daily use rather than display-case fragility.

Materials are stainless steel and titanium steel. Both resist tarnishing and corrosion, which is the practical reason so many wholesale jewelry programs have moved away from plated brass. Plated items look fine out of the polybag and generate returns six weeks later once the plating wears at the high-contact points. Steel doesn't have that failure mode, and it holds up through shipping, handling and repeated wear — a meaningful factor when you're buying in volume and shipping internationally.

Finishing is where the retro character comes from. The surface carries an oxidized treatment that darkens the recesses and produces that aged, vintage look, while the edges are polished so the piece doesn't read as simply dirty or unfinished. A subtle texture runs across the face as well. The combination is what makes the pendant work across the casual, gothic and minimalist styling mentioned in the product description — the oxidized body does the rugged half, the polished edges do the refined half.

## Checklist Before You Commit

A few things are worth pinning down before you sign off on a bulk order.

Confirm the physical specs in writing against the specific SKU — pendant height and width, chain length, weight per unit, clasp type. Close variants in a metal jewelry line can differ more than the product photos suggest. It's also fair to ask about finish durability, since the oxidized treatment is the visual signature here; ask how consistently the polishing is applied across a batch, and whether a sample can be provided to check it firsthand.

Settle the sample question early. For a 12-piece in-stock order, the pieces themselves function as the sample. For a 1,200-piece custom run, request a pre-production sample of your engraved or packaged version before the full run goes ahead — that's when corrections are cheap.

Then confirm the commercial terms: unit pricing at your target quantity, packaging format, and the customization scope you need quoted. Bulk pricing is tiered by quantity, so state your expected annual volume rather than a single order if you plan to reorder.

One more item worth raising: mixed containers. If you're also buying rosaries, scapulars or medals, ask whether the pendant can ship alongside them. Consolidating freight across a religious goods order is one of the easier costs to control.
