## Material and Design Highlights

Metal construction is the foundation here, and it matters more than buyers sometimes expect. A keychain lives in a pocket or bag alongside coins and keys, so the finish takes abrasion constantly. The polished surface on this piece is meant to hold its appearance through that cycle rather than dull after a few weeks of use.

The crystal embellishments are the detail that decides whether the product survives a season of retail. Setting quality, not stone count, is what keeps them in place — a loose stone turns a saleable item into a return. If you're sourcing for a shop where customers inspect before they buy, ask how the stones are secured and request a handling sample to test yourself.

Blue is a deliberate choice, too. Silver and gold crosses are everywhere in religious gift catalogs, so a blue finish gives you something to merchandise differently — a color story for a display, a matching note for a confirmation group, a visual break on a pegboard. It reads as devotional without looking like a piece of fashion jewelry.

Size is compact, which is the right call for a carry item, though it does mean the cross is a detail rather than a centerpiece. That suits the unisex brief. Engraved or not, it works on a man's carabiner and a woman's handbag strap without looking borrowed from the other.

## What to Verify Before Ordering

Specifications first. Exact dimensions and unit weight aren't published for this item, so contact us to confirm before you commit to a display fixture, a shipping carton count, or a landed cost calculation. Guessing on either end of that is how margins disappear.

Then the components you can't see in a photo. Split ring thickness and spring tension matter more than buyers expect — a thin ring that bends out of shape generates complaints. Finish adhesion is the other one; ask whether the plating or coating is specified for abrasion resistance, and how the supplier tests it.

Sampling is the step most buyers skip when timelines tighten, and it's the one that catches problems. Request a physical sample and put it on a keyring for a week. Check for stone movement, edge sharpness on the cross, and finish wear at the ring contact point. That's a better test than any photo set.

Finally, paperwork. Import requirements for metal accessories and crystal-set items vary by destination market, and we won't claim certifications that aren't documented. Tell us your market and what your customs broker or compliance team needs, and we'll confirm what documentation is available for this SKU — and flag anything that isn't.
