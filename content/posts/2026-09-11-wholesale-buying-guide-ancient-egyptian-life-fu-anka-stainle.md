## Material and Design Highlights

The pendant body is high-grade stainless steel with silver plating. For a wholesale buyer, that combination answers three questions at once: how will it photograph, how will it wear, and how many will come back as returns. Stainless steel holds fine engraving well, so the totem lines and the ankh outline stay crisp rather than softening into a blur. It also resists tarnish, which means the piece keeps its silver tone on a pegboard or in a warehouse without the maintenance headaches that come with softer base metals.

Hypoallergenic construction matters more than it sounds. Pendants sit against skin all day, and skin sensitivity is one of the quieter drivers of customer complaints in costume jewelry. Stainless steel with silver plating sidesteps much of that risk and supports daily wear.

The aesthetic itself is deliberately timeworn. A polished mirror finish would make the totem detail disappear; the aged patina gives the engravings contrast and depth, so the motif reads clearly from a distance and rewards a closer look. That's the kind of detail that sells in photographs, which is where most of your customers will meet the product first.

Are you sourcing for a store that already carries Egyptian or esoteric themes? This piece slots into that shelf without looking like an outlier. For a church-oriented shop, it works as a companion item to devotional jewelry rather than a replacement for it — the ankh carries enough archaeological and spiritual weight to sit near religious accessories comfortably.

## What to Verify Before Ordering

Before you commit to a container or even a carton, pin down the details that aren't visible in a product photo. Some of these Mecrt publishes directly; others you'll want to confirm in writing.

**Physical specifications.** Pendant dimensions, metal thickness, chain or cord gauge, and the clasp or bail type are all things to request as a spec sheet. These aren't listed on the product page, so contact us to confirm what's standard and what can be adjusted.

**Plating and finish.** Ask how the silver plating is applied and what wear life is expected under normal use. The aged patina is a finishing effect, so batch-to-batch variation is normal — request photos of an actual production unit, not just the studio shot, so you know what range to expect.

**Sampling.** A pre-production sample is the cheapest insurance in this business. Wear it, photograph it, hand it to a staff member for a week. If the finish or weight doesn't match your shelf, you'll know before the bulk run, not after.

**Packaging format.** Poly bag, header card, gift box, or something custom with your logo. Confirm what's included at the base price and what's an add-on, because packaging can shift your landed cost more than the pendant itself.

**Order terms.** Confirm current pricing tiers, lead times and shipping terms for your destination, plus any documentation your market requires for imported jewelry. Availability and documentation requirements vary by item and by country — ask rather than assume.
