## Ivory, Resin, and Bulk Are Three Different Decisions

A search phrase like "ivory resin bead traditional catholic sacrament rosary bulk" usually compresses three separate requirements into one line: an ivory appearance, a resin material, and a wholesale quantity. Each one needs its own answer on a quotation. Buyers who treat the phrase as a single specification often approve a sample that matches the color but not the material, or confirm a material at a quantity the supplier cannot actually produce.

Catholic rosaries are used across baptism, First Communion, Confirmation, weddings, memorials, daily prayer, and rosary groups. That range is why the same phrase can describe very different orders. A parish buying for a sacramental preparation program and a distributor building a year-round catalog may both say "ivory," but they are rarely asking for the same bead size, cord construction, or packaging.

Before requesting pricing, decide which parts of the phrase are fixed. If ivory is a color requirement and the material is open, you have more sourcing room. If resin is a fixed material requirement — perhaps because the end customer wants a non-wood bead — state that explicitly. Material is an item-level question, and availability varies by item, so it should be confirmed against the exact product you plan to order rather than assumed from a category description.

## Bead Size and Cord Construction Drive Most Reorders

Size is the specification buyers most often leave loose, and it is the one that generates the most back-and-forth after a quotation. Common rosary bead sizes are 6 mm, 8 mm, 10 mm, and 12 mm, and availability varies by item. Do not assume that a size list shown on one listing applies to every rosary in a supplier's range. When you request a quote, name the size you need and ask which items in the catalog can be produced or supplied in it.

Cord construction matters just as much for cord rosaries. Peruvian knots and flat knots are both available for relevant cord rosaries, but the knot type must be confirmed for the specific item you select. If your brief specifies a knot style — because a customer, a parish, or an existing sample calls for it — put that in the enquiry rather than in a later revision. Changing knot construction after a sample is approved usually means starting the approval process again.

Two checks worth building into every enquiry:

- Ask the supplier to confirm the bead size and knot type in writing for the exact item quoted.
- Ask whether the crucifix and centerpiece shown in the listing photo are the ones that ship, or whether they vary by item.

## Resin Finish Versus Natural Wood: What the Quotation Should State

Ivory resin and natural wood are not interchangeable in production terms, and the difference should be visible in the specification rather than buried in a product title.

If your brief calls for a wood rosary, note that our relevant wooden rosaries use natural solid wood rather than plastic or resin imitation wood, and the beads are hand polished. That distinction matters to buyers whose customers handle a rosary regularly and expect a natural material with visible grain. It also matters when a quotation comes back cheaper than expected: imitation wood is a different material, so confirm which one the price reflects.

## MOQ, OEM Tags, and Church or Parish Branding

Quantity planning usually comes down to one question: are you buying existing stock, or commissioning a customized production run? The commercial terms differ.

In-stock orders carry a minimum order quantity of 12 pieces. That suits parishes, schools, and small ministries buying for a specific event or group, and it allows you to evaluate the item before committing to a larger program.

OEM and custom-logo orders carry a minimum order quantity of 1,200 pieces. For these orders, a logo, church name, or parish name can be customized on eligible tags or packaging. Two points are worth clarifying early, because they affect both cost and approval:

- Not every tag or packaging format is eligible for customization — confirm the specific tag or packaging you have in mind.
- Customization applies to tags and packaging, not to the rosary itself.

If your organization needs the customization to match a diocesan style guide or an existing parish identity, send the artwork or wording with the enquiry so eligibility can be confirmed at the same time as the quantity and item.
