## Specs and Build Quality

The construction story here is short and honest. Natural wooden beads, oval-pattern shaping, a cross that stays firmly attached, and enough structural integrity for repeated use. That last point matters more than it sounds. A rosary that falls apart after a month of daily prayer creates complaints, replacements and administrative hassle — and for a parish, that's a far bigger cost than a few cents per unit.

The oval bead profile is the detail buyers tend to notice first. Round beads roll and slide; a flatter oval sits steadier between thumb and forefinger, particularly for older parishioners or anyone praying the rosary while walking. Combined with the organic texture of unfinished or lightly finished wood, it holds a tactile quality that molded plastic simply doesn't replicate.

## Checklist Before You Commit

Run through this before you sign off on a bulk order, whether it's 12 pieces or 12,000.

- **Sample first.** Request a physical unit. Check bead size against your target user, confirm the cross stays tight, and test the string or cord tension yourself.
- **Confirm the spec in writing.** Bead material, bead shape, cross style and finish, overall length. Get it on the quotation.
- **Ask about finish.** Whether the wood is raw, waxed or lacquered affects feel and longevity. It varies by item, so confirm.
- **Check packaging options.** Bulk bag, individual bag, or carded — and whether you need barcodes or retail labels applied.
- **Clarify logo method and placement.** For OEM orders, confirm artwork format, where the mark goes, and whether it's on the cross, a tag, or packaging.
- **Get the lead time in writing.** Production and shipping windows vary by quantity and customization level.
- **Ask what documentation ships with the order.** Export paperwork, labeling and compliance needs differ by destination market. Tell us where it's going and we'll confirm what's available.
- **Request the volume price break.** Pricing shifts as quantity climbs — get the tiers for your realistic annual draw, not just your first order.

That last point deserves emphasis. A buyer who plans to reorder quarterly should be quoting against the annual figure, not the opening shipment. It changes the conversation.
