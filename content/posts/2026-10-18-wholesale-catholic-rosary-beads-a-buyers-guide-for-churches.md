## What to Confirm with the Supplier

Product listings describe intent; a purchase order describes obligation. Before you commit to a bulk run, get the specification sheet in writing and check it against your sample.

Start with materials. The source material confirms "durable materials" but doesn't break down bead composition, pendant metal or plating, or cord and chain construction. Those details drive your landed cost and your customer complaints, so contact Mecrt for specifications rather than assuming. Bead diameter, overall length, pendant dimensions and unit weight should all be documented — you'll need them for freight calculations and for any marketplace listing.

Then examine a physical sample. Check the pendant's casting detail and edge finish, the uniformity of the bead polish, the tension of the string or chain, and whether the centerpiece sits straight. Photographs hide a lot; a sample in your hand doesn't.

Packaging deserves its own line item. Confirm whether units ship individually bagged, boxed or bulk-packed, what the inner and outer carton quantities are, and whether retail-ready packaging is available for your channel. If your market requires labeling or documentation, raise it early — requirements vary by destination and by item, so ask rather than presume.

Finally, settle commercial terms: pricing at your target volume, production and delivery timing for both in-stock and OEM orders, and the sample approval process. Neither price nor lead time is fixed across this category; both vary by specification and quantity, so request a quote against your actual numbers instead of working from a general figure.
