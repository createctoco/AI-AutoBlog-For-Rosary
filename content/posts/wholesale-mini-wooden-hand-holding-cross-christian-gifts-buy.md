## Customization and OEM Options

Mecrt offers customization on bulk orders, specifically engraving and packaging adjustments. For a religious gift like this, those two levers cover most of what buyers actually ask for.

Engraving is the higher-value option. A parish name, a date, a scripture reference, or a short dedication turns a general devotional item into a commemorative piece for a jubilee, ordination, or school graduation — and commemorative items typically carry better margins than stock goods. The supplier's core lines already support logo customization, so the conversation is a practical one, not a hypothetical.

Packaging adjustments matter more than they sound. Switching from a plain poly bag to a printed card-backed header, a small gift box, or an organza pouch changes where the product can sit — a boxed version can go to gift retail, while a bulk-packed version suits kit assembly. If your channel needs a barcode label, a hang tag, or multi-language text, raise it early.

Two thresholds govern the conversation. In-stock orders start at 12 pieces, which is a sensible way to test the item before committing. OEM and custom work starts at 1,200 pieces. Between those two numbers you have a fair amount of room — a small first order to validate sell-through, then a custom run once you know the item earns its shelf space.

## What to Confirm with the Supplier

Before you commit to a bulk order, get the physical details in writing. The source material doesn't publish dimensions or weight, so ask for both — you'll need them for carton planning, freight estimates, and any marketplace listing.

Wood species and finish deserve the same treatment. Natural wood varies, and buyers running multi-store programs usually want consistency between shipments. Ask how the pieces are sanded and sealed, and whether color variance between batches is controlled or simply inherent to the material.

On engraving, confirm the available area, the method used, and what happens to the artwork approval process — who signs off, and at what stage changes stop being free.

Wondering about pricing structure? Request a quote with your target quantity and ask whether tiered pricing applies at higher volumes. The source doesn't publish a price list, so a direct quotation against your specific order size is the only way to get a real number. Same for lead time: it varies by order type and customization level, so confirm it against your delivery window rather than assuming.

Finally, ask about packaging format, carton configuration, and any documentation your destination market requires on import. Get a sample before the bulk run — a single piece in hand settles questions about scale, finish, and feel faster than any spec sheet.
