## Specs and Build Quality

The bracelet is made with genuine turquoise beads, each one selected for natural color variation. That's the character of the material and it's what separates a stone bracelet from a plastic one — but it's also something you need to brief your buyers on before a pallet lands. No two pieces match exactly. If your channel expects uniform color, plan for that conversation now, or ask about a tighter bead sort when you order.

Construction is a woven cord with an adjustable closure rather than a metal clasp. From a quality standpoint, that's a smart trade for this price tier: there's no clasp component to fail, nothing to spec in terms of plating thickness or spring tension, and nothing to corrode. The woven cord is the load-bearing element, so the knot tension and cord quality are what you should be looking at in a sample.

Traditional weaving techniques carry through the design, which keeps the piece feeling handmade rather than machine-assembled. Bead spacing, cord thickness and the finish of the weave all affect how the bracelet drapes on the wrist. Those are the details worth putting under a lamp before you commit to volume — photographs rarely show them honestly.

Where specs aren't listed publicly, don't guess. Bead diameter, cord material, exact adjustment range and piece weight all vary by item and production run. Contact us for the current specification sheet if you need firm numbers for a listing, a customs document, or a retail planogram.

## Checklist Before You Commit

A short list of things to verify, in roughly the order they'll bite you if you skip them.

**Request physical samples first.** Photos won't tell you how the weave feels or whether the adjuster holds tension under daily wear. Wear the sample for a week before you approve it.

**Confirm bead specs in writing.** Diameter, drill consistency and color sort. If you need a tighter or looser color variation, say so at the sampling stage — it's a bead-selection question and it's easier to resolve early.

**Test the adjustment mechanism.** Slide it through its full range several times. You want a cord that grips firmly but still moves smoothly, without fraying at the friction point.

**Lock down packaging.** Poly bag, display card, branded pouch, gift box — each changes your landed cost and your shelf presence. Get the packaging spec attached to the purchase order.

**Ask about the current production schedule.** Lead times vary by item and order volume, and packaging customization adds a step. Don't build a launch date on an assumed timeline; ask.

**Check your labeling obligations.** Country of origin marking and any importer details your market requires should be settled before production, not after.
