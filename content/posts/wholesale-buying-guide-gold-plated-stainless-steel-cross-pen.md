## Materials and Craftsmanship

The base metal is stainless steel, which matters more than the finish when you're buying at volume. Stainless resists tarnish and corrosion, so pendants don't dull in a warehouse or in a display case between buying seasons. It's also hypoallergenic — a practical point for church and school group orders, where the buyer may be distributing dozens of pieces to people they don't know personally, and nobody wants a skin reaction to become the story of the event.

Gold plating over that base gives the piece its warm, gift-appropriate look while keeping the underlying durability intact. The product description calls out a polished finish with a subtle luster, which is a sensible choice for this market. Highly reflective gold plate can photograph well online but can look sharp in person under fluorescent church lighting; a softer polish tends to read as restrained and appropriate for devotional wear.

Construction is described in clean terms — a simple cross form, no ornate filigree, nothing that catches on fabric or collects tarnish in tight crevices. That simplicity is a manufacturing advantage too. Fewer surface details mean fewer places for plating defects to hide, and QC inspection is faster because the visual criteria are unambiguous.

One thing worth flagging: the source material doesn't specify pendant dimensions, chain length, chain style, or plating thickness. Those details vary by item and by production run, so contact us to confirm the exact specifications for the lot you're considering before you publish measurements on your own storefront or print a catalog. Getting the numbers directly is faster than guessing from a photo.
