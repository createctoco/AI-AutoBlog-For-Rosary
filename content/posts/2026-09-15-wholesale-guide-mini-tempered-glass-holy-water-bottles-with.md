## Specs and Build Quality

Tempered glass is the material decision that matters most here. Compared with standard glass, tempered glass handles repeated handling and the small knocks that come with being carried around, which counts when your end customer is a pilgrim or a child receiving a first holy water bottle.

The transparency is the design choice doing the heavy lifting. Nothing hides the contents — the holy water stays visible through the body — and the miniature rose is worked so the glass itself carries the detail rather than a printed label or plastic insert. That's the difference between an item that reads as a religious novelty and one that reads as a handicraft.

Size is deliberately small, which is the entire point of the format. Pocket and bag portability is the design brief, so buyers should think accessory first and vessel second. Exact capacity, outer dimensions, cap construction and unit weight aren't uniform across every version — contact us to confirm the specification sheet for the variant you're considering.

Durability sits in the same conversation. Tempered glass improves the survival rate through packing, shipping and gifting cycles, but packaging still does a lot of work for a glass item moving in bulk cartons. Ask how units are nested or sleeved inside the master carton before you commit to a container load or an air shipment.

## Checklist Before You Commit

Samples first. A product photo tells you the shape; a physical unit tells you the weight, the fit of the cap, and whether the rose detail holds up at the size you're buying. Request a sample of the stock version before signing off on a custom run.

Confirm the specification in writing: capacity, dimensions, glass treatment, cap material and seal, unit weight. A holy water bottle lives or dies on whether the closure holds, so ask directly how the cap performs after filling and during transport. If your market requires it, confirm whether the item ships empty and how that's labelled.

Then work through the commercial side. What packaging will the custom order use, and does the 1,200-piece MOQ cover your packaging choice or move with it? What's the lead time on a first custom run versus a repeat order? Which colour, size and logo options exist within the custom programme? Those answers vary by item and by order, so treat the written quote as your reference point rather than anything assumed.

Finally, match the SKU to the channel. A pilgrimage organiser wants low unit cost and simple bulk packaging. A gift shop wants retail-ready presentation at a price point that survives two markups. Those are different orders off the same product, and telling the supplier which one you are gets you a more useful quote.
