## Construction and Materials

The rosary's defining feature is straightforward: 10mm natural wood beads. That's a comfortable size for prayer — big enough to feel substantial in the hand, light enough to carry in a pocket or bag. The beads are smooth and polished, which matters for a rosary that gets handled daily. A rough or splintering bead is a fast way to disappoint a customer; polished wood avoids that problem entirely.

The St Benedict medal is what sets this piece apart. It carries long-standing protective symbolism in Catholic tradition, and it's a detail buyers specifically look for when sourcing devotional items. Pairing the medal with a detailed cross means the rosary covers both core devotional elements without you having to source them separately.

The metal components — the crucifix and the medal — are finished to resist tarnish. That's a genuine selling point for institutional buyers. A rosary that discolors after a few weeks of use creates returns and complaints, and tarnish resistance is exactly the kind of spec you want confirmed in writing before committing to a bulk order.

Behind the scenes, production relies on traditional hand-knotting techniques. Mecrt works with an artisan team of over 160 Peruvian knot craftsmen, producing more than 100,000 pieces monthly. That's not a marketing flourish — hand-knotting affects how the rosary feels, how it drapes, and how durable it is over time. It also matters for consistency: when you order hundreds or thousands of units, every piece needs to feel like the sample you approved.

## Pre-Order Verification

Before you place a bulk order, a few things are worth confirming directly with Mecrt. The source material gives you the headline specs — 10mm wood beads, St Benedict medal, tarnish-resistant metal — but your purchase order should rest on exact numbers.

Ask for a full specification sheet: bead count per rosary, total length, weight per unit, and the specific metal alloy used for the crucifix and medal. The tarnish-resistance claim should come with a description of the finish, so you know exactly what you're promising your own customers. If you're selling into a particular market, check whether the materials meet any import or labeling requirements you're subject to.

Sampling is your friend here. Request a physical sample or a pre-production sample, especially if you're going the OEM route. Handmade products vary; even with skilled artisans, a sample tells you what actual production consistency looks like. Confirm the MOQ for your specific configuration too — the 12-piece threshold applies to in-stock items, and the 1,200-piece minimum to custom work. Your exact terms will depend on quantities, packaging, and any modifications.

Lead times, packaging options, and payment terms are all things Mecrt can confirm in writing. And if you're buying for a specific event — a pilgrimage season, a confirmation cycle, a diocesan gathering — make those dates clear in your first inquiry so the supplier can tell you whether they can realistically meet them.
