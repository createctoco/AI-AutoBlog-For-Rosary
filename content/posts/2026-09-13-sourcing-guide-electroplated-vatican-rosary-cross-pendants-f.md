## What to Verify Before Ordering

Metal components vary more than buyers expect, so a short verification list saves a lot of back-and-forth later.

Start with the physical spec: overall length, width, thickness, and the size of the hanging loop or attachment point. If you're threading cord or attaching jump rings, that opening dimension decides whether the part works at all in your assembly, so confirm it in writing rather than assuming.

Next, the finish. Ask how the plating behaves across a production run — whether color is consistent batch to batch, and how it's expected to hold up under normal wear. If your product spends time on a display rack or in humid retail space, that question is worth asking directly.

Then there's the sample. Ordering a sample before a bulk commitment is standard practice for a reason: a photo can't tell you how a crucifix feels in the hand or whether the scale suits the beads you plan to pair it with. Compare the sample against your existing inventory before you sign anything off.

Commercial terms deserve the same scrutiny. Confirm the MOQ that applies to your specific configuration, since a custom finish may sit at the OEM threshold rather than the stock one. Ask about production lead time, packing method, and shipping terms — these vary by order size and destination, so get them confirmed against your own timeline rather than relying on general figures. Export experience helps here: Mecrt already ships to Europe, North America, and South America.

Finally, if you need retail-ready packaging — branded bags, barcodes, printed cards — raise it at quotation stage. It affects both your unit cost and your schedule.
