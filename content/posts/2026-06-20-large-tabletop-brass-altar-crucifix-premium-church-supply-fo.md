## Why Church Buyers Keep Coming Back for Our Brass Altar Crucifixes

We’ve been making altar crucifixes for years. Not the flimsy stamped-metal kind you find at discount stores. We’re talking heavy brass, solid construction, the kind that sits on a tabletop and doesn’t tip over when someone brushes past it during a procession.

You’d be surprised how many churches order a crucifix once, then call us six months later because the finish started peeling. That doesn’t happen with our pieces. We cast them properly, hand-polish them, and check every single unit before it goes out the door.

### The Size Question — Bigger Isn’t Always Better

Here’s something we’ve learned dealing with parish buyers: everyone thinks they need a massive crucifix. A 24-inch brass piece on a 12-inch base sounds impressive. But when it arrives, half the time it overwhelms a smaller altar or looks awkward next to the tabernacle.

We offer tabletop crucifixes in several sizes. Our 12-inch and 15-inch heights sell best for side altars and chapel use. The 18-inch works for main altars in medium-sized churches. Anything larger? That’s typically a floor-standing processional cross, not a tabletop piece.

One thing we insist on: the base needs to be heavy enough. A top-heavy crucifix is a hazard waiting to happen. Our brass bases are weighted and have non-slip felt pads underneath. No wobbling. No sliding.

### What You Get When You Order From Us

Let me walk you through what’s actually in the box.

First, the brass itself — we use C26000 cartridge brass, not the cheap recycled alloy that gets brittle over time. The surface gets a high-polish finish, then a clear lacquer coat to prevent tarnishing. If your church prefers an antique bronze look, we can do that too. Just specify at order.

The corpus — the body of Christ — is cast separately and attached by hand. We don’t use glue that dries out after a year. It’s mechanically fastened. You can pick a traditional Gothic-style corpus or a more modern smooth design. Both are detailed, not blobby.

And the cross? No hollow tubes. Solid brass tubing or cast brass, depending on the model. The arms are joined with hidden tenons, not visible seams.

### A Few Practical Notes

If you’re ordering for a church, here’s what I’d suggest:

- Measure your altar table first. A 15-inch crucifix on a 10-inch-deep altar looks cramped.
- Consider the wall behind it. Dark wood paneling? A polished brass crucifix pops beautifully. White walls? Maybe go with the antique bronze finish.
- Think about the seasons. Lent calls for a veiled crucifix in some traditions. Make sure the base is stable enough to hold a veil without tipping.
