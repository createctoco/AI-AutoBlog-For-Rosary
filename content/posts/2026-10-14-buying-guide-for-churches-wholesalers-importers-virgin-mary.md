## Construction and Materials

Stainless steel is doing a lot of work here. It resists tarnish, it holds a polish well, and it survives the handling that comes with high-turnover displays — the kind of countertop rack where pieces get picked up, put back, and picked up again. If your inventory sits behind glass and turns slowly, that durability matters less. For a parish running a fundraiser table or a distributor packing event giveaways, it matters a great deal.

Then there's the pave work. Small zircon stones set tightly together create the sparkle that separates this piece from a plain cast pendant, and they also create the quality-control question every buyer should ask about. Pave settings live or die on tolerances. If stones aren't seated properly, a few will work loose in transit or in the first weeks of wear, and returns on jewelry almost always trace back to the setting or the clasp. Ask your supplier how the stones are secured and how their QC line checks retention.

A lobster clasp is a small detail with an outsized effect on the customer experience. It's more secure than a spring ring for everyday wear, and it's easy for a customer to operate without help — relevant if you're selling to older parishioners or through a gift shop with no try-on space.

The design itself is a contrast play: a sacred silhouette in polished metal, ringed with brilliant detailing. Compact, wearable, and it doesn't look like a souvenir. That's the combination that gets a religious pendant onto a customer who isn't shopping in a religious store.

## Pre-Order Verification

Samples first, always. Photographs of pave jewelry flatter the stones, and you want to judge sparkle, metal finish and weight in hand before committing to a bulk run.

When the sample arrives, check a few specific things. Run a fingernail across the stone field to feel for raised or loose settings. Work the lobster clasp open and closed a dozen times. Inspect the polish under direct light for swirl marks or dull patches. And confirm one basic point the product naming leaves open — whether the item ships as a complete necklace or pendant only. Order the wrong configuration in bulk and you'll be repacking at your own cost.

Beyond the sample, get the commercial details in writing before you commit. What's the current MOQ for your chosen configuration? What's the lead time on in-stock versus custom orders? What's the packaging spec and carton dimension? And if your market regulates nickel release or requires material composition statements, ask what documentation is available instead of assuming it exists. These details vary by item and order size, so confirm them directly with the supplier.
