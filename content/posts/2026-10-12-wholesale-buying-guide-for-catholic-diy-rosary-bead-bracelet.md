## Material and Design Highlights

Two components carry the product: the beads and the string.

PVC is the workhorse. It's lightweight yet sturdy, which matters twice over — once at the assembly table, where a bead that cracks under pressure ruins the kit, and once at the freight stage, where weight per carton drives your landed cost. PVC also takes color well enough to support repeat production runs, though batch-to-batch consistency is something you'll want confirmed in writing.

The alloy string provides the structure. It's what keeps the bracelet from stretching out of shape after repeated assembly, and it's the part most worth stress-testing when you evaluate a sample. Alloy quality varies between suppliers; the difference rarely shows on day one but shows up quickly in a customer's hands.

Diamond-cut detailing is where the design earns its keep. The faceted surface catches light in a way plain beads don't, so the finished bracelet reads as a gift item rather than a craft supply. That's often the difference between a piece a church shop can place in a display case and one that sits in a bulk bin.

Elastic construction deserves a mention too. No clasps to fail, no sizing to manage, no returns over fit. For a wholesale buyer, that's less after-sales friction and a simpler SKU to manage across seasons.

## What to Verify Before Ordering

Sample first. Request a pre-production sample in the exact bead color and string length you're considering, then assemble a few bracelets yourself before signing off. Your hands will find issues a spec sheet won't.

After that, work through the details that decide whether a reorder goes smoothly:

- **Bead finish and color consistency** — ask how color is matched across production batches, since PVC can drift between runs.
- **Elastic tension** — confirm the stretch holds after repeated assembly and wearing.
- **Alloy string grade and finish** — base metal and plating aren't stated on the product page, so contact us to confirm what's used for your order.
- **Packaging format** — flat-packed, bagged, or boxed changes your landed cost and handling time.
- **Logo and label placement** — if you're branding the package, confirm artwork format and exactly where the mark goes.
- **Lead time and shipping terms** — these vary by item and volume; ask for your specific timeline rather than assuming a standard.
- **Partial shipment policy** — worth pinning down in writing on large custom runs.
- **Sample-to-bulk tolerance** — agree on how much color variation is acceptable before the order is confirmed.
