## St Benedict Catholic Protection Medal Pendant: What Wholesale Buyers Need to Know

We get a lot of questions about the St Benedict medal. Not just the pendant itself, but how it fits with rosaries, scapulars, and other devotional items. So let’s talk about it.

The St Benedict medal is one of the most recognized Catholic sacramentals. It’s not a charm or a good luck token — it’s a symbol of protection, rooted in the Benedictine tradition. The cross on the back, the letters around the edge (V.R.S.N.S.M.V. – S.M.Q.L.I.V.B.), the exorcism formula… there’s a lot of history packed into a small piece of metal.

But if you’re a wholesaler, importer, or church buyer, you’re probably thinking about something else: *Where do I get these in bulk, at a fair price, with decent quality?*

We make them. Not just the medals, but the whole assembly — chain, pendant, packaging, the works.

Honestly? Consistency.

Our St Benedict medals are die-struck, not cast. That means the details are sharp — the cross, the letters, the image of St Benedict holding the cross and the Rule. The finish is uniform. No rough edges.

### Size Options: 8mm vs 10mm vs 6mm

The 8mm size outsells everything else by a mile. It’s big enough to handle comfortably during prayer, small enough to fit in a pocket or a gift box.

10mm comes close second. Feels more substantial, especially for older hands.

6mm? That’s for the tiny rosaries — the ones people wear as a bracelet or carry in a purse. We make those too, but the demand is lower.

12mm is available but it’s niche. Usually for large wall rosaries or processional use.

The St Benedict pendant itself? We make it in standard sizes — about 2.5cm to 3.5cm diameter. Big enough to read the details, not so big it looks like a medallion.

### The Peruvian Knot vs Flat Knot

On our rosaries, we offer two knot options:

- **Peruvian knot** — tight, uniform, traditional. Holds the beads firmly in place.
- **Flat knot** — simpler, lies flatter against the chain.

Most Catholic buyers prefer Peruvian knot. It’s more secure and looks cleaner. But we’ve done flat knot for some Eastern Catholic parishes — they liked the look better.

Either way, we don’t use glue. The knots hold the beads. That’s how it should be.

### Where Does the St Benedict Medal Fit?

Practical use? People wear it around the neck. They attach it to a rosary. They put it in a car or on a doorframe.
